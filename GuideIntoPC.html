Guide Into PC's

Before Researching Or Purchasing Any Parts, It Is Important To Understand Each Component of A PC To Better Plan Your Build

First, What Is A PC?

A PC is a system of parts that each perform a different job:

  • One part thinks (CPU)
  • One part remembers things (RAM))
  • One part stores everything long-term (SSD/HDD))
  • One part shows visuals (GPU)
  • One part powers everything (PSU)
  • One part holds everything together (Motherboard + Case)

Depending on your desired use of your PC, you would allocate more or less into these certain areas.

Now we will look a bit deeper into each part and their main factors

The Importance of the CPU:

What It Does:

The CPU is the brains of the operation, the core that determines how smooth normal programs will operate. When not performing extensive tasks like gaming and editing, the CPU handles the tasks

Factors:

When looking into a CPU, the three main factors are how many Cores does the CPU have: This determines the amount of tasks it handle at once, the Threading: which is the power behind the CPU and what it can handle, and the Clock Speed labeled in Ghz: which tells us how fast these processes can occur.

Consider:

The general advice is that 6 cores is good for most tasks and moderate gaming. However for heavier tasks, such as streaming, or handling multiple moderate operations at the same time with no hiccups, then 8 cores or more will be your bet.

The Importance of the GPU:

What it does:
  • Renders games
  • Handles video editing
  • Powers monitors
Important:
  • NOT required for basic PCs (CPU can do basic graphics)
  • REQUIRED for gaming or design work
Key terms:
  • VRAM = video memory (important for games)
  • More VRAM = better for high-resolution gaming

The Importance of the RAM:

What it does:
  • Short-term working memory.
  • Helps multitasking
  • Keeps apps open
Important:
  • RAM does NOT store files permanently
Key Sizes:
  • 8GB = minimum (barely OK today)
  • 16GB = sweet spot for gaming/general use
  • 32GB+ = heavy work (editing, engineering)

The Importance of the SSD/HDD:

SSD (Solid State Drive):
  • Fast.
  • Modern standard
  • Boots PC in seconds
HDD (Hard Drive)
  • Slower
  • Cheaper
  • Good for large file storage
Recommendation:
  • Always get SSD first
  • 500GB–1TB is typical beginner range

The Importance of the Motherboard:

What it does:
  • This connects everything.
  • Lets CPU, RAM, GPU communicate
  • Provides USB ports, audio, internet
Key idea:
  • You MUST match motherboard with CPU type.
  • Intel CPU → Intel motherboard
  • AMD CPU → AMD motherboard

The Importance of the PSU (Power Supply Unit):

What it does:
  • Converts wall electricity into usable power
Important rules:
  • NEVER cheap out here
  • Must have enough wattage
  • Mid gaming PC: 550W–750W

The Importance of the Case and Cooling:

What matters:
  • Airflow (cooling)
  • Size compatibility (ATX, Micro-ATX, etc.)
Cooling:
  • Keeps CPU/GPU from overheating.
  • Air cooling (fans + heatsink)
  • Liquid cooling (advanced, expensive)

TLDR

Basic use (school, browsing)
  • No GPU needed
  • 8–16GB RAM
  • SSD
  • Low-power CPU
Gaming PC
  • Dedicated GPU required
  • 16GB RAM minimum
  • SSD and HDD
  • Strong CPU
Editing / streaming
  • Strong CPU + GPU
  • At least 1 TB SSD and HDD
  • 32GB RAM recommended
